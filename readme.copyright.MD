Its name pays homage to Kenji Miyazawa’s Ihatov, a poetic vision of an idealized world. Just as Miyazawa imagined Ihatov as a place where dream and soil coexist, the IHATOV Engine is designed to hold both — the imaginative dreamscapes of visual novels and the grounded textures of comics, films, and interactive worlds.

At its core, IHATOV is not just a piece of software. It is a philosophy of continuity: stories should flow fractally, branching and recombining, while remaining true to their essence. It is a cultural substrate, a studio architecture, and a meta‑game framework all at once.

How It Works

The IHATOV Engine operates on a fractal narrative system. Instead of scripting one medium at a time, creators define nodes — scenes, characters, moods, and events — in a unified format.





Visual novel simulation: The engine can output sequential dialogue and scene prompts, like a traditional VN.



Fractal narrative encoding: Each node can be expanded or collapsed, producing detail for a film shot list or abstraction for a comic panel.



Cross‑media continuity: A single character line can appear as a speech bubble in a comic, a subtitle in a film, or a caption in a book.



Procedural adaptation: The system automatically adjusts layouts, pacing, and style depending on the medium preset chosen.

This means creators don’t have to “port” their work from one medium to another. The IHATOV Engine renders the same story across multiple forms simultaneously.

Games: Interactive worlds where player attention shapes outcomes, echoing the simulator you saw with Kenji choosing Dream or Soil.



Comics: Panel layouts generated from scene nodes, with dialogue mapped into speech bubbles and fractal grids.



Film: Shot lists and cinematic ratios derived from the same narrative substrate, producing storyboard sequences automatically.



Books: Chapters and prose generated from the same nodes, formatted for print or digital reading.



Music and sound: Emotional states encoded as motifs, producing adaptive soundtracks across games and films.

The power of IHATOV lies in its cross‑domain universality. A single creative act — writing a scene — can ripple outward into comics, films, games, and books without loss of integrity.

The IHATOV Engine represents a new paradigm in cultural production.





Efficiency: Creators no longer need separate pipelines for each medium. One substrate, many outputs.



Integrity: Characters and worlds remain consistent across adaptations. No more “lost in translation” moments.

Meta‑game awareness: IHATOV doesn’t just tell stories — it reflects on the conditions of storytelling itself.
